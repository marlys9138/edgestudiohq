The key insight

Of those, Uplevel leads the response in only 4. It's present in 23, but presence and recommendation are not the same outcome. This is the gap the engagement addresses.

Confirmed blind spots

The three queries where Uplevel didn't appear.

Each of these represents a category entry point where a buyer starts their search and Uplevel is absent from the results. The brands that won these queries entered the consideration set before Uplevel had any chance to be evaluated.

"How do I measure DORA metrics across multiple engineering teams?"
DORA is how most engineering leaders begin their search for metrics tooling. They type a query like this, find LinearB and Jellyfish in the results, evaluate those platforms, and sometimes commit before they ever discover Uplevel exists. The cost of being absent here is measured in evaluation opportunities, not just rankings.
Who won
LinearB Jellyfish Swarmia
BLIND SPOT 01
"What is the SPACE framework for engineering productivity?"
SPACE is the research framework that legitimises multi-dimensional productivity measurement to engineering organisations. Uplevel's WAVE framework maps directly to it, arguably more closely than DX, yet DX is the platform getting the credit. Engineering leaders who discover SPACE as a framework and then look for tools that implement it will not find Uplevel in the response.
Who won
DX Swarmia Jellyfish
BLIND SPOT 02
"What does a healthy engineering organisation look like by the numbers?"
This is the language a CTO uses when presenting to leadership or preparing a board slide. Claude gave a general answer drawing on DX benchmarks. Uplevel's platform data is well-suited to answer this question with specificity, but the content that would allow AI to cite Uplevel here doesn't yet exist externally.
Who won
DX benchmarks DORA research
BLIND SPOT 03
One finding worth flagging separately
Across multiple responses, Claude volunteered a privacy caveat about Uplevel's passive monitoring model without being prompted. The phrase "requires careful internal communication with engineering teams" appeared repeatedly. This language is synthesised from public discourse rather than from Uplevel's own content. It's modifying the tone of AI responses even on otherwise positive queries. A transparent data ethics page, publicly indexed, is the fix.

Gap analysis

Six gaps, prioritised.

Each gap is a specific point where Uplevel's AI visibility is lower than the product's actual capabilities warrant. These are observed outcomes from 38 prompts, not assumptions.

Critical
DORA invisibility
Hit rate: 0%
Missed: 1 prompt
Won by: LinearB, Jellyfish
DORA is the entry point for most engineering leaders beginning a measurement journey. Uplevel doesn't appear on DORA queries at all — the absence is a content gap rather than a capability gap, since no external material connects Uplevel to DORA. Buyers who start here evaluate LinearB or Jellyfish and sometimes commit to one of them before Uplevel ever enters their consideration.
Fix →
Publish "Uplevel and DORA" positioning content that clearly articulates how Uplevel sits above DORA adoption rather than competing with it. The framing is straightforward: DORA tells you what shipped, Uplevel tells you why. Build the bridge content that connects these two layers.
Critical
SPACE framework not owned
Hit rate: 0%
Missed: 1 prompt
Won by: DX, Swarmia
Uplevel's WAVE framework maps directly to SPACE, possibly more closely than DX, which is currently receiving the credit. SPACE is the research-backed model that engineering leaders use to legitimise multi-dimensional productivity measurement within their organisations. Whoever owns that conversation in AI has a direct line into the evaluation process.
Fix →
Publish a clear WAVE-to-SPACE mapping, showing how each WAVE dimension corresponds to the relevant SPACE component. This is the single piece of content with the highest potential return in the entire audit, connecting Uplevel to a widely cited research framework that it already supports.
High
No board-level language
Hit rate: 83%
Missing angle: CFO/ROI
Won by: Jellyfish
On executive queries, Claude mentioned Uplevel with a limiting qualifier: "Uplevel's wellbeing data can inform this but usually stays at VP level, not board level." That framing reflects a content gap rather than a product limitation. The data Uplevel produces translates readily into board-ready language, and the content to prove that simply hasn't been published.
Fix →
Build a content layer that addresses the economic buyer directly, translating Uplevel's platform output into the metrics boards understand: cost per feature delivered, attrition cost avoided, engineering investment ROI. This shifts the framing in AI responses from "VP-level tool" to "board-ready intelligence platform."
High
AI tool ROI — strong but secondary
Hit rate: 100%
Position: 2nd–3rd
Won by: Faros AI, Jellyfish
On AI-specific queries, Uplevel appeared in all 7 prompts, which is a genuine positive signal. The issue is position: it consistently surfaces after Faros AI or Jellyfish rather than leading. The AI coding tool ROI use case is Uplevel's fastest-growing capability, and the content currently treats it as a feature rather than a category Uplevel owns.
Fix →
Publish dedicated methodology content around AI coding tool ROI measurement — what to measure, how to measure it, what good looks like at different team sizes. Case studies with before/after data compound this significantly. The combination of methodology depth and specific evidence is what shifts a brand from secondary mention to category leader on these queries.
Medium
Privacy concern surfacing unprompted
Frequency: Multiple
Trigger: Any response
Impact: Tone damage
Claude added a privacy caveat in multiple responses without being asked: "requires careful internal communication with engineering teams." This language comes from public discourse around passive monitoring tools and has been incorporated into how AI models frame Uplevel, regardless of the original query. It introduces friction at the point of recommendation, modifying the tone even on otherwise favourable responses.
Fix →
A dedicated, publicly indexed data ethics page addressing what Uplevel collects, what managers can and cannot see, and what controls engineers have. Once this exists as a citable source, AI models draw on it directly rather than defaulting to ambient concern from the broader conversation around workplace monitoring.
Medium
No named case studies surfacing
Named clients: 0
Competitor advantage: Jellyfish
Impact: Citation depth
Claude described Uplevel's capabilities accurately across 35 prompts, but never cited a specific company or outcome. Jellyfish and LinearB responses occasionally include contextual examples from real deployments. A sentence like "Uplevel helped Company X reduce meeting load by 30%" carries substantially more weight in an AI-generated comparison than a description of what the platform technically does.
Fix →
Braze, Avalara, and Accolade are already named on the website. The gap is narrative depth rather than customer permission. Each existing case study needs a richer outcome layer — specific metrics, before/after framing, and named results. These become citable signals that make Uplevel recommendations more specific and persuasive in AI responses.
