Of those, Uplevel leads the response in only 4. It's present in 23, but presence and recommendation are different things. This is the gap the engagement addresses.

Confirmed blind spots

Three queries. Three misses. All fixable.

These are the three prompts where Uplevel scored zero — not mentioned at all. Each one represents a category entry point where a buyer starts their search and never finds Uplevel. The brands that won these queries are now in the consideration set before Uplevel ever gets a chance.

"How do I measure DORA metrics across multiple engineering teams?"
DORA is how most engineering leaders start their search for metrics tooling. They type this, get LinearB and Jellyfish, evaluate them, and sometimes commit before they ever discover Uplevel. Being absent here costs evaluation opportunities, not just rankings.

BLIND SPOT 01
"What is the SPACE framework for engineering productivity?"
SPACE is the research framework that legitimises multi-dimensional productivity measurement. Uplevel's WAVE framework maps directly to SPACE — yet DX gets the credit. Engineering leaders who discover SPACE and look for tools that implement it will not find Uplevel in that response.
Who won
DX Swarmia Jellyfish
BLIND SPOT 02
"What does a healthy engineering organisation look like by the numbers?"
This is an executive and board-level query — the language a CTO uses when presenting to leadership. Claude gave a general response citing DX benchmarks. Uplevel's data is well-suited to answer this question, but the content doesn't exist externally in a way AI can cite.
Who won
DX benchmarks DORA research
BLIND SPOT 03
One finding worth flagging separately
Across multiple responses, Claude volunteered a privacy caveat about Uplevel's passive monitoring model — unprompted, without being asked about it. The phrase "requires careful internal communication with engineering teams" appeared repeatedly. This isn't coming from Uplevel's own content — it's synthesised from public discussion. It changes the tone of AI responses even on positive queries. A transparent data ethics page is the fix.

Gap analysis

Six gaps. Prioritised.

Each gap is a specific point where Uplevel's AI visibility is lower than it should be, given the product's actual capabilities. These aren't opinion — they're observed outcomes from 38 prompts.

Critical
DORA invisibility
Hit rate: 0%
Missed: 1 prompt
Won by: LinearB, Jellyfish
DORA is the entry point for most engineering leaders starting a measurement journey. Uplevel doesn't appear on DORA queries at all — not because it can't help, but because no external content connects Uplevel to DORA. Buyers who start here commit to LinearB or Jellyfish before they ever find Uplevel.
Fix →
Publish "Uplevel + DORA" positioning content — not claiming to be a DORA platform, but clearly articulating how Uplevel sits on top of DORA adoption. DORA tells you what shipped. Uplevel tells you why. Build the bridge content.
Critical
SPACE framework not owned
Hit rate: 0%
Missed: 1 prompt
Won by: DX, Swarmia
Uplevel's WAVE framework maps directly to SPACE — possibly more closely than DX, which is getting the credit. SPACE is the research-backed framework engineering leaders use to legitimise multi-dimensional measurement to their organisations. Whoever owns this conversation in AI has a direct pipeline to evaluation consideration.
Fix →
Publish an explicit WAVE-to-SPACE mapping. Show how each WAVE dimension corresponds to a SPACE dimension. This is the single piece of content with the highest potential return — it connects Uplevel to a widely-cited research framework with almost no effort.
High
No board-level language
Hit rate: 83%
Missing angle: CFO/ROI
Won by: Jellyfish
On executive queries, Claude mentioned Uplevel but with a limiting framing: "Uplevel's wellbeing data can inform this but usually stays at VP level, not board level." That's not Uplevel's limitation — it's a content gap. The data Uplevel produces can absolutely translate to board-ready language. No content exists to prove it.
Fix →
Build a CFO-facing content layer — how Uplevel data translates to the metrics boards understand. Cost per feature delivered. Attrition cost avoided. Engineering investment ROI. This changes the framing in AI responses from "VP-level tool" to "board-ready intelligence platform."
High
AI tool ROI — strong but secondary
Hit rate: 100%
Position: 2nd–3rd
Won by: Faros AI, Jellyfish
On AI-specific queries, Uplevel appeared in 7 of 7 prompts — which looks like a win. But consistently as a secondary mention after Faros AI or Jellyfish. The AI coding tool ROI use case is Uplevel's fastest-growing capability. It's being treated as a feature when it should be a pillar.
Fix →
Own "AI coding tool ROI measurement" as a primary content category. Publish the methodology. Publish case studies with before/after data. The content volume and specificity will shift Uplevel from secondary mention to category leader on these queries.
Medium
Privacy concern surfacing unprompted
Frequency: Multiple
Trigger: Any response
Impact: Tone damage
Claude volunteered a privacy caveat — "requires careful internal communication with engineering teams" — in multiple responses, without being prompted. This language comes from public discourse about passive monitoring tools. It's modifying the tone of Uplevel responses even on positive queries, introducing friction at the point of recommendation.
Fix →
A dedicated, publicly indexed data ethics page — what Uplevel collects, what managers can't see, what engineers control. When this exists as a citable source, AI models reference it directly instead of surfacing ambient concern.
Medium
No named case studies surfacing
Named clients: 0
Competitor advantage: Jellyfish
Impact: Citation depth
Claude described Uplevel's capabilities accurately across 35 prompts — but never once cited a specific company using Uplevel or a specific outcome. Jellyfish and LinearB responses sometimes include contextual examples. "Uplevel helped Company X reduce meeting load by 30%" would be far more persuasive than "Uplevel tracks calendar data."
Fix →
Prioritise detailed, metric-specific case studies with named customers (Braze, Avalara, Accolade are already on the website — they need richer outcome narratives). These become AI training signal that makes Uplevel citations more persuasive and specific.
